Plastic Waste Guide

Rigid plastic containers vs soft film plastic.

What CAN go in the yellow bin

  • Plastic water and soft drink bottles (PET #1)
  • Milk jugs and detergent bottles (HDPE #2)
  • Rigid food containers and punnets
  • Shampoo and body wash bottles, rinsed

What CANNOT go in the yellow bin

  • Plastic shopping bags and soft wrappers
  • Polystyrene foam boxes and meat trays
  • Plastic cutlery and straws
  • Garden hoses and PVC piping

Tip: Empty liquids, rinse off food and screw caps back on. Soft plastics advisory: if it scrunches, keep it out of the yellow bin and use a soft plastics collection where one is available.

Paper & Cardboard Guide

Clean paper, boxes and cartons.

Recyclable

  • Flattened delivery boxes and cereal boxes
  • Newspapers, magazines and office paper
  • Milk and juice cartons
  • Paper bags and greeting cards (no foil or glitter)

Not recyclable

  • Greasy pizza box bases and food wrappers
  • Used paper towels, napkins and tissues
  • Waxed paper and thermal receipts
  • Takeaway coffee cups

Tip: Flatten boxes completely and keep paper dry, because wet fibres break down and cannot be remade into paper.

Glass Bottles & Jars Guide

Food and drink containers only.

Accepted containers

  • Clear, green and brown drink bottles
  • Jam, sauce and pickle jars
  • Wine and beer bottles
  • Glass condiment containers, rinsed

Prohibited glass

  • Drinking glasses and vases
  • Window glass and mirrors
  • Pyrex and other heat-resistant cookware
  • Ceramics and broken glass

Tip: Non-container glass melts at a different temperature and can ruin a whole batch of recycled glass. Wrap broken pieces securely before putting them in the red bin.

Metal & Aluminium Guide

Aluminium cans, steel tins and clean foil.

Accepted metals

  • Aluminium drink cans
  • Steel food tins, rinsed
  • Clean foil, scrunched into a golf-ball-sized ball
  • Clean foil trays

Keep out of the yellow bin

  • Gas bottles, which can explode in trucks
  • Pots, pans and scrap metal
  • Wire coat hangers
  • Paint tins that are not empty

Tip: Empty liquids and rinse tins to prevent odours. Do not crush cans, because sorting machines identify them by shape.

E-Waste & Batteries Guide

Batteries, phones, computers and appliances.

Take to a drop-off point

  • AA, AAA and button batteries, with terminals taped
  • Lithium-ion batteries and power banks
  • Phones, tablets, laptops and accessories
  • TVs, printers, cables and small appliances

Never do this

  • Putting any battery in a household bin (fire hazard)
  • Leaving TVs or computers out on the kerb
  • Throwing away a device without wiping your data
  • Keeping swollen or damaged batteries indoors

Tip: E-waste contains valuable metals and hazardous substances. Lithium batteries in bins and trucks are a growing cause of fires.

Organics (FOGO) Guide

Food organics and garden organics.

Accepted in the green bin

  • Fruit and vegetable scraps
  • Cooked food, meat and bones
  • Coffee grounds and tea leaves
  • Grass clippings, leaves and prunings

Not accepted

  • Plastic bags, including "degradable" ones
  • Pet waste and kitty litter
  • Treated timber, soil and rocks
  • Nappies

Tip: FOGO services vary between councils. If yours does not offer one, home composting works for most scraps. Do not add meat to home compost.

General Waste Guide

What belongs in the red landfill bin.

Red bin items

  • Soft plastics, where no collection is available
  • Nappies, wipes and face masks
  • Polystyrene foam and cling wrap
  • Broken crockery, wrapped securely

Never in the red bin

  • Batteries and e-waste
  • Paint, chemicals and motor oil
  • Gas bottles
  • Medicines (return these to a pharmacy)

Tip: Check the search directory before you bin something. Many items that look like rubbish have a dedicated recycling or drop-off option.
