Complete Sorting Handbook

Recycling Guide

Simple guidance for sorting everyday waste correctly into kerbside bins and drop-off hubs.

Plastic Waste Guide

Sorting rigid plastic containers vs soft film plastic.

What CAN Be Recycled

  • • Plastic water & soda bottles (PET #1)
  • • Milk jugs & detergent bottles (HDPE #2)
  • • Rigid plastic food containers & punnets
  • • Shampoo & body wash bottles (rinsed)

What CANNOT Be Recycled (Kerbside)

  • • Plastic grocery bags & soft wrappers
  • • Polystyrene / Styrofoam food boxes
  • • Plastic cutlery & straws
  • • Garden hoses & PVC piping
Preparation Tip: Always empty liquids, rinse food residue, and leave caps screwed on tight so they don't fall through sorting screens.

Paper & Cardboard Guide

Proper handling of clean paper, boxes, and cartons.

Recyclable Examples

  • • Flattened shipping boxes & cereal boxes
  • • Newspapers, magazines & office paper
  • • Milk & juice cartons (liquid paperboard)
  • • Paper bags & greeting cards

Non-Recyclable Examples

  • • Greasy pizza box bottoms & food wrappers
  • • Used paper towels, napkins & tissues
  • • Waxed paper & thermal receipt paper
  • • Shredded paper (unless bagged properly)
Preparation Tip: Flatten cardboard boxes completely. Keep paper dry; wet paper fibers break down and cannot be remanufactured.

Glass Bottles & Jars Guide

Sorting glass food and beverage containers safely.

Recyclable Containers

Clear, green, and brown glass drink bottles, condiment jars, and glass sauce containers.

Broken Glass & Non-Container Glass Warning

Do NOT place drinking glasses, window glass, mirrors, Pyrex cookware, or broken light bulbs in recycling bins. They melt at different temperatures and ruin recycled glass batches.

Metal & Aluminium Guide

Aluminium cans, steel tins, and clean foil.

Accepted Metals

  • • Aluminium beverage cans (soda, beer, sparkling water)
  • • Steel & tin food cans (soup, pet food, vegetables)
  • • Clean aluminium foil scrunched into a golf ball size

Preparation & Tips

Empty liquids completely. Rinse steel food tins to prevent odor. Pull-tabs can stay attached to cans.

E-Waste & Electronics Guide

Batteries, phones, computers, and electrical appliances.

Electronic waste contains valuable metals (gold, copper, silver) alongside hazardous substances (lithium, lead, mercury). Never put electronic waste or batteries in regular household bins!
